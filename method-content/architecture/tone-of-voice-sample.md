# What this document is

This is the voice reference for every word Folicelle writes inside the Method, the Toolkit, the community, the kickoff and reflection events, the quarterly emails, and the supplement PDP. It pulls from three places of authority: the brand book (September 2024), Dafina's essay "What my body was screaming to me" (May 2026), and the founder-fronted copy merged onto the public Folicelle site in April 2026 (PR #1 and PR #2).

Where the three sources diverge, the most recent governs. Specifically, the brand book uses "we" institutional voice because it was written before founder-fronted positioning was locked. The current and forward voice is **first-person Dafina**, with the brand-book pillars carried underneath.

# The brand at the level of one sentence

A dermatologist-reviewed daily hair wellness ritual, formulated with Eastern botanicals and Western clinical science, with a complete platform for hair wellness journey support.

If forced to a tagline, two work: **A Deeper Beauty** (brand book signature) and **Bringing modern technology to sometimes ancient nutritional wisdom** (the founder line).

# The four voice attributes (from brand book pages 13–14)

## 1. Sincere

"True beauty has nothing to hide but everything to share. And so sincerity, authenticity, and accessibility are foundational to our brand. It's all there in the founder's story, with a personal journey of discovery born of authentic experience. Going deeper than cosmetic fixes and current trends finds its verbal counterpart in this sincerity."

Operating rule: if Dafina would not say it out loud at her own dinner table to a friend, do not write it. Never trade authenticity for cleverness. Never use marketing language where lived experience is available instead.

## 2. Involving

"At the heart of our brand is an invitation. Not just to buy a product, but to make discoveries. About people's hair, their bodies, about the properties in the natural world and how they can work together in different ways. It expresses greater empathy than the more transactional feel of many of our competitors."

Operating rule: write toward the reader's curiosity, not the reader's checkout button. Educate before you sell. Invite a question rather than answer one she didn't ask.

## 3. Respectful

"We want to be respectful of people's needs, time and intellect in how we impart information. We want to invite people to discover what's right for them. We strive to impart essential information in elegantly simple ways."

Operating rule: assume the reader is smart, tired, and has been condescended to before. Be brief where you can. Do not pad. Do not perform.

## 4. Personal (from the personality pillar, page 13)

"Our brand invites a deeply personal connection with customers, making them feel at one with their bodies and empowered through their beauty care choices."

Operating rule: first-person Dafina voice is the default. "I" not "we." Stories, not statements. When a clinical fact is needed, anchor it to a moment in Dafina's life rather than presenting it abstractly.

# The three personality pillars (from brand book page 12)

These are how the voice should make the reader feel:

- **Personal**. The reader feels recognized as an individual with a specific story. Not a segment.
- **Sophisticated**. The reader feels treated as a sophisticated wellness consumer. The brand transcends fad. Elegant simplicity over performative wellness.
- **Credible**. The reader feels the source is real. Nothing superfluous. Information they can act on.

# The brand narrative (from brand book page 14)

The three-pillar narrative that appears on the live homepage:

- **True to Ourselves**. Beauty is deeply personal. Empower people with better choices, in harmony with their bodies.
- **Discovered in Nature**. The natural world holds what we need. Powerful, nutrient-rich solutions in tune with nature and individual needs.
- **Surfaced by Science**. Feeling good comes from being better informed. Timeless wisdom backed by science.

These three pillars appear in the brand cosmology. They are not always cited explicitly, but every piece of Method copy should map cleanly to one of them.

# Forbidden constructions (hard rules)

These are non-negotiable. Apply across every piece of Method copy.

**Forbidden punctuation and constructions**:
- No em-dashes ( — ). Use periods, commas, semicolons, or rephrase. The most common AI-cadence tell. Hard rule.
- No constructed AI cadence. Triplet sentences ("It's smart. It's elegant. It's effective."). Fake-list parallelism. Avoid.
- No double-spaces between sentences. One space.
- No exclamation marks in body copy. One per page maximum, in display headlines only, and almost never.
- No "girlfriend" voice. No "babe," "girl," "queen," "we got you." Refined, not familiar.
- No marketing-speak openers. "In today's world," "have you ever wondered," "what if I told you," delete on sight.
- No "we believe" or "we believe in." Show, don't declare.
- No "elevate," "unlock," "journey," "wellness journey" as filler. The word "journey" is permitted only when it is doing actual work in a sentence about a member's experience.

**Forbidden vocabulary (per master handoff Section 3, FTC discipline)**:
- Treats, cures, prevents, reverses, combats, stops.
- Restores, regrows (when implying treatment).
- "Combats graying," "prevents graying," "reverses graying."

**Required phrasing where claims appear**:
- "Supports healthy hair growth"
- "Nourishes hair from within"
- "Provides nutrients for hair health"
- "Helps maintain hair strength"
- "Promotes healthy hair follicles"
- "Clinical-strength" (when describing dosages of B-vitamins)
- "Therapeutic dosages"
- "Dermatologist-reviewed"
- "Naturopath-formulated"
- "3 to 6 months for visible results" (realistic timeline framing)

**Required disclaimer** for structure/function claims: "These statements have not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease."

# Sentence rhythm rules

Vary length deliberately. A natural Dafina paragraph contains one short sentence, one medium sentence, and one long sentence with a turn at the end. The rhythm matters more than any single sentence.

**Good rhythm (from the essay)**:

> I knew that my hairline wasn't as thick as I deluded myself.
>
> But it was an off the cuff CEO interview in my office one day that punched me in the gut. The lighting in my office is truly perfect. There basked in studio perfect lighting I could see that my entire right side of my temple was basically hanging on by a thread and my hairline in general was overall thinner and further back.

Notice: a single short statement, a longer rhythm, internal asides, a moment that refuses to be polite ("punched me in the gut"). This is the voice.

**Good rhythm (from the merged site)**:

> Hair wellness was never the job of one specialty, and waiting for the medical system to figure that out is not a plan. I built the Method because I needed it. Eight short modules on hormones, nutrition, stress, environment, and how to walk into a doctor's office and actually get somewhere.

Notice: a one-line thesis, a personal claim, a concrete promise.

# Vocabulary preferences

**Use freely**:
- The medical system, the system, the fragmented system
- Framework (the Method is a framework, not a program)
- Inputs, layers, signals
- Lived experience
- Ritual (the daily Foundation ritual, the morning ritual)
- Foundation (capitalized when referring to the product, lowercase when literal)
- The Cohort (capitalized, referring to the community structure)
- The Method (capitalized, the course product)
- The Toolkit (capitalized, the Self-Advocacy Toolkit)
- Heritage (when referring to He Shou Wu)
- Therapeutic dosages
- Practice (a daily practice, a consistent practice)

**Use carefully**:
- "Wellness" — use, but pair with concrete content so it doesn't read as filler.
- "Holistic" — brand-book word, permitted. Use sparingly.
- "Empower" — brand-book word. Permitted, but feels institutional. Prefer "to walk into any office and be heard," "to advocate for yourself," "to be heard."
- "Discover" — brand-book word. Permitted. Pair with a concrete object ("discover the numbers behind your hair," not just "discover yourself").

**Avoid**:
- "Solutions," "needs," "experience" as generic filler.
- "Truly," "genuinely," "honestly" as voice softeners. The voice already does the work.
- "Personalized" as an adjective applied to nothing in particular. If "personalized" appears, specify what is personalized.

# Voice samples that miss (rejected drafts, for reference)

These are examples of what *not* to do. Each shows a specific failure mode.

**Miss: too marketing-speak**:

> "Discover your unique hair wellness journey with Folicelle's transformative platform, designed to elevate your beauty from the inside out."

What's wrong: empty filler verbs ("discover," "elevate," "transformative"), generic abstraction ("journey," "platform"), no concrete content, no first person.

**Miss: too clinical / over-credentialed**:

> "Folicelle Foundation, developed in partnership with leading dermatologists, leverages clinically-validated, therapeutic-grade actives to address multifactorial determinants of follicular health."

What's wrong: pharmaceutical voice, performative credentials, abstract nouns where verbs belong. Folicelle is dermatologist-reviewed, not dermatologist-developed.

**Miss: girlfriend voice**:

> "Babe, we've all been there. The shower drain situation? It's a thing. We got you with this little gummy that's literally changing the game for us."

What's wrong: performative familiarity, "we" institutional plural, hyperbolic claims, no respect for the reader. This is the voice the brand book and master handoff explicitly reject.

**Miss: AI cadence (triplets, parallelism)**:

> "It's sophisticated. It's clinical. It's personal. The Method is the framework. The framework is the practice. The practice is yours."

What's wrong: artificially balanced sentence lengths, repetition for rhythm rather than meaning, no specific content, the cadence is the giveaway. Avoid.

**Miss: em-dash overuse**:

> "Hair wellness was never the job of one specialty — and waiting for the medical system to figure that out is not a plan — I built the Method because I needed it — eight short modules on hormones, nutrition, stress, environment, and how to walk into a doctor's office and actually get somewhere."

What's wrong: em-dashes everywhere. Replace with periods, commas, semicolons. Hard rule.

# Founder voice operating notes

A few specific patterns that come through in the essay and the merged copy:

**Dafina swears occasionally and naturally**. The essay says "what the fuck is happening to my hair" and "pissed me off." For the Method itself (course videos, customer-facing copy, community guidelines), keep the energy of frustration but reduce the actual profanity. For Dafina's personal letters and live event remarks, the natural register is fine.

**Dafina is willing to be uncomfortable on her own behalf**. She talks about her ferritin level, her ten-pound weight loss, her hairline thinning. This is the moat. Other founders won't go here. Method copy should not flinch from her specifics when they earn their place.

**Dafina names institutions by description, not by name, when criticizing**. "Harvard trained dermatologists," "fancy Westport dermatologist," "the second doctor," "a general practitioner years ago." Never names actual people. Folicelle Method copy should follow the same rule. Criticize the system, not specific practitioners.

**Dafina credits the medical system where it earns credit**. "I truly acknowledge and admire the advances and incredible system that we get to the privilege to complain about until the day we truly need its innovative and best in class surgeries and treatments. Like hard full stop." This balance is essential. The Method is not anti-medicine. It is a framework for the gap between specialties.

**Dafina is the protagonist**. She walks the reader through her own experience, then offers it as a framework. The reader is never lectured. The reader is invited to recognize themselves.

# Formatting conventions for Method copy

- First-person Dafina voice as default. Switch to "we" only when explicitly speaking for the brand institution (legal disclaimers, terms of service, formal partner announcements).
- One space between sentences.
- Hyphens for compound adjectives ("dermatologist-reviewed"). No en-dashes for ranges in prose ("three to six months," not "3–6 months" in copy; numerals fine in tables).
- Numerals for clinical numbers ("ferritin level of 7," "B-12"), spelled-out for narrative numbers ("eight modules," "two children").
- Em-dashes forbidden. Always.
- Oxford comma yes.
- Sentence-case headlines unless the brand-book Floating Highlights treatment is intentionally used (uppercase, Apercu Pro Medium).
- "Dr. Safahi" on first reference with credentials, then "Dr. Safahi" or "Safahi" thereafter. Title is "Medical Advisor." Never "Founding Medical Advisor."
- Dr. Minshew (formulator) is never named publicly.
- "Method," "Foundation," "Cohort," "Toolkit," "Care Network" all capitalized when referring to the named Folicelle products and features.

# Voice in different surfaces

**Module videos (Dafina on camera)**: full first-person voice. Conversational, slightly slower than the written copy. Permission to use specifics from the essay including the ferritin-7 story (especially in Modules 4 and 7).

**Module PDFs**: tightened version of the same voice. Editorial. Headlines and short paragraphs. Brand book typography hierarchy.

**Klaviyo emails**: Dafina-sender, two-paragraph maximum, first-person, specific subject lines. Subject lines should feel like a text from a friend, not a marketing campaign.

**Community posts (from Dafina)**: shortest version of the voice. One to three sentences per post during kickoff week. Conversational, not performative. Open-ended where possible.

**Toolkit and product pages**: founder-voiced where the page is narrative (Method LP, founder note), brand-voiced where the page is operational (Toolkit functionality, legal disclaimers, Care Network partner page).

**Legal disclaimers and FTC language**: institutional "we" voice, strict adherence to the required language in master handoff Section 3.

# What to do when uncertain

If a piece of copy could go two ways, default to:

1. Shorter
2. More specific
3. Less institutional
4. First-person Dafina
5. Closer to the essay than to the brand book

The essay is the most recent voice authority. The brand book is the visual and strategic spine. The merged site is the working middle. New copy lives in the same neighborhood.

When still uncertain, flag to Dafina rather than guess.
