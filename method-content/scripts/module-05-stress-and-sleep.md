# Cold open (~20 seconds)

Most of the women I know have been told that their hair is probably stress.

What no one tells you is that the stress that is shedding your hair right now is the stress you were carrying three to four months ago. Your hair is on a delay. The month you fell apart in shows up in your shower drain in spring.

This module is short. It is the lever you can pull tomorrow morning.

*B-roll suggestion: a slow pan of a darkened bedroom in late afternoon, a glass of water on a nightstand, a single hair on a pillow. Title card: "Module 5, Stress and Sleep."*

# Segment 1, The three-month delay (~2 minutes)

I want you to picture the worst month of your last year.

Maybe it was a death in the family. Maybe a divorce. Maybe a job loss. Maybe a postpartum that broke you for a while. Maybe a stretch of insomnia you do not want to talk about. Whatever it was, picture it.

Three to four months after that month, your follicles cycled. A higher-than-usual percentage of them moved into the resting phase together. Two to three months after that, those resting hairs let go. The shed you noticed in the shower five or six months after the worst month is the follicle's record of what your body was holding.

This is called telogen effluvium and we name it in Module 1. The reason the name matters is that the timing is unintuitive. Most women blame the current month for the current shed. The current shed is the past month catching up. The opposite is also true. The work you do this week to reduce cortisol is not going to show up in your hair for months. Hair is a long, slow scoreboard.

This is also why one bad month does not ruin your hair forever. The follicle cycles. The shed releases the past. New growth shows up. The mistake is assuming that nothing is happening because you cannot see the change today.

*B-roll suggestion: a calendar with one month circled in pen, then a slow zoom out showing four months passing, then a wide shot of a calm bathroom. The visual is patience, not crisis.*

# Segment 2, Sleep is a hair input (~2 minutes)

Sleep is not a luxury for hair. It is one of the four inputs we talked about in Module 1, hidden inside the others. Sleep is when the follicle does its repair work, the way every other cell in your body does its repair work overnight.

Two specific things happen during sleep that the follicle cares about. The first is that cortisol drops to its lowest level of the day. The second is that growth hormone, which is involved in tissue repair, runs through its largest daily release in the first few hours of deep sleep. Miss the deep sleep window, and you miss the work.

I am not going to tell you to sleep eight hours. You know that. I am going to tell you what actually moves the needle for the women I have talked to about this.

The most underrated input is consistency. Same bedtime, same wake time, weekdays and weekends. Cortisol responds to consistency more than it responds to duration. If you are sleeping six hours but at the same time every day, that is more useful to your hair than seven hours that shift by ninety minutes across the week.

The second input is the hour before bed. Not the meditation app. The hour of light, screens, food, and stimulation that precedes the sleep. If you can lower the light, leave the phone in another room, and not eat in the last sixty to ninety minutes, the deep sleep gets deeper. The work the follicle does in that window does not require you to be a wellness influencer. It requires the windows to be honored.

The third input is what you do about your stress between bedtimes. Stress does not have to be eliminated. It cannot be. What it has to be is metabolized. Walks. Therapy. Conversations with people you love. Whatever your version of metabolizing it is, more of that. Letting cortisol live in your body unprocessed is the input that shows up in your hair four months later.

# Segment 3, The honest sentence (~1 minute)

I want to be honest with you for a second.

I cannot fix your stress through a module. I cannot make your postpartum easier through a course. I cannot get your work to email you less. I am not a sleep coach.

What I can do is name the relationship between what your life looks like and what your hair does, so that you stop blaming the shower drain for what is actually a chronicle of your year. The shed is not the problem. The shed is the record.

When the record is bad, what we change is the year. Not the hair.

The most powerful thing I did the year my ferritin came back at seven was give my body the inputs it had been asking for. Not because I wanted to grow back the temple I had lost. I did. But the temple was the alarm, not the fire. The fire was a body that had been running on undersupply, under-sleep, and under-attention for a long time, while it raised twins, started a company, lost a parent, and built a brand. The fire was my life.

I am still living that life. So are you. The work is not to escape it. The work is to read the signal it gives you.

If you take one thing from this module, take this. The shed is the record, not the problem. Change the record by changing the inputs.
